Versatile · three directions

What the audit found

Walked the running app at 1440×900 in both themes (login → workspace → editor → Chapters, Consistency, Network, Settings). The system is disciplined and well documented; its problem is not inconsistency, it is that nothing is figure and nothing is ground. Eight findings, ranked. Each direction below is scored against them.

A1The manuscript has no more presence than the chrome

Sidebar, header, canvas and docked panel are three greys within 6 L% of each other (--vers-bg-base #121214, panel #1a1a1d, elevated #26262b) separated by 7 % hairlines. The prose sits on the same surface as the navigation. For a writing tool, the page should be the brightest, most material object on screen.

A2The one accent is spent on two buttons

Slate blue appears on the primary button and the active nav row, and nowhere else. "One accent, scarce" is right; but scarce is not absent. Saved state, the caret, the goal bar, links and progress have no voice, so the app reads as inert even while it is saving, counting and scanning.

A3Every empty state is the same empty state

Icon in a grey circle · title · one line · a button, identically in Workspace, the editor, Chapters, Consistency and Network. Five identical templates in a first session is the strongest "generated" signal in the product (EmptyState.vue).

B1Type has no hierarchy above 14 px

Panel titles, section headings, the project name and the workspace heading are all Geist 13–16 px, weight 500–600. The only typographic personality in the app is the tracked-mono wordmark on the login screen, and it never appears again.

B2The manuscript reads as a terminal, not a page

IBM Plex Mono on a dark grey field with no margin, rule or sheet. Mono can be a signature (it is a real writers' preference), but it needs a page around it: a margin, a running head, a caret with colour, a scene marker.

B3The docked panel is the sidebar's twin

Same colour, same hairline, same 13 px labels on both sides of the manuscript. The eye cannot tell "navigation" from "tool". The Story Network header's three coloured pills are the only chroma on the screen and they are decorative.

C1Status is grey

"Saved", the word count, the daily goal and the streak are 11 px muted text in the header. The semantic tokens exist (--vers-status-*) and are barely used outside evals.

C2Light theme is a value swap, not a mode

Cream paper is a good instinct, but the same flatness carries over: no sheet, no shadow, the manuscript again on the same surface as everything else. A light mode for a writing app should feel like paper under a lamp; today it feels like the dark mode inverted.

How to choose

Toggle the theme on each: the direction you pick has to work in both. B is the smallest change that fixes the flatness (tokens plus one surface) and keeps the mono manuscript everyone already writes in. A is the biggest shift in feel: it turns Versatile from a tool into a desk, and it makes the light theme the real one. C is the most distinctive and the most polarising; it is the direction the login screen was already pointing at. Once you pick, I lock it into DESIGN.md + style.css tokens, re-skin the 15 primitives (stories and Chromatic catch regressions), and the enhancement study re-renders itself.
